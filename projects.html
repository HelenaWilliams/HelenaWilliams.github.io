Demo Projects

iD Tech Curricula

At iD Tech, instructors are expected to maintain a focus on teaching through project development. Since not every student is prepared to take on a top-level project (let alone come up with an idea for one), I have created my own library of demo projects that I can guide students in creating. These range from simplistic games to much more complex challenges.

Pong

Pong serves as an excellent introduction to Pygame for students who like math. With some tinkering, this game is able to provide introductions to a number of subjects, such as:

  • Physics: How do we make a ball move with realistic elastic collisions in 2D? Ricocheting off of walls and paddles is an essential part of Pong, and may not be immediately intuitive to novice programmers. I typically break down the problem into two one-dimensional number lines, using conditionals to switch the ball's trajectory when it hits a wall. More advanced students may be capable of discussing trigonometry and precise angles, but it's not necessary.

  • Cartesian Plane: How do we detect essential boundaries in the game--for instance, the sides of a paddle, or the goal zone? Younger students may not have prior knowledge of the coordinate plane, and Pong allows me to show them how coordinates change in an intuitive manner. I also use this as a chance to explain inequalities, through demarcating regions of the display ("bounce areas," "score areas," etc.) that need different behaviors.

  • Heuristics How do we make an AI that is engaging to play against? We can easily draw a second paddle, but making it capable of responding in an "intelligent" manner is much more difficult. I start this lesson with an "easy mode": a paddle that drifts up and down, without accounting for the movement of the ball. From there, students learn to develop towards a "medium mode" paddle that tries to follow the y-coordinate of the ball, and even a "hard mode" paddle that tries to calculate the ultimate trajectory of the ball.

  • Code Structure How do we write legible and functioning code? Pong doesn't necessitate object-oriented programming, or multiple code files, but different parts of the game are best divided into separate functions, all of which are called from a main "game loop." I use this to teach about passing parameters, calling functions, variable scope, and other essential concepts for designing more modular code.
